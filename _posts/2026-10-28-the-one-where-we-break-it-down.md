date: 2026-10-30

description: Not every business problem needs more people or resources.
  Sometimes, asking the right questions and breaking things down can reveal what
  really needs to change.
---
As businesses grow, problems become more complex, and the most obvious solution isn't always the right one. Consider a founder overwhelmed by daily decisions who thinks it's time to hire more people, but asking why the workload keeps piling up, why decisions require their attention, and why employees can't make those decisions themselves might reveal that the real issue is unclear responsibilities and approval processes. This is the idea behind the **5 Whys**, a root cause analysis technique that helps uncover underlying problems by repeatedly asking "why" rather than simply addressing the symptoms.

The 5 Whys is one of the tools used in the **Analyze** phase of **DMAIC**, a Six Sigma framework with five stages: **Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, and Control**. Each stage serves a specific purpose, with **gate checks** to review findings before moving forward, helping teams address problems systematically rather than rushing into solutions. **The Plus Side?** *Sometimes, improving a business isn't about adding more people, resources, or effort, but breaking down the problem, asking the right questions, and understanding what actually needs to change.*
